# Synthetic case study: Pinehaven restricted-fund control

This case study illustrates the governed workflow; all names, amounts, identifiers, and events are fictional.

## Before

Pinehaven's accounting, bank evidence, project support, and governing authority existed in separate systems. A proposed **$6,800 operating-payroll transaction** was associated with a restricted capital-fund cash source.

## MFCL analysis

The restricted-funds workflow connected the proposed transaction to its Economic Event ID, funding source, purpose, accounting period, project, budget, and available authority evidence.

## Detected issue

The synthetic authority permitted capital purposes. Routine operating payroll did not align with that purpose. The fact that cash was available did not establish legal or accounting eligibility.

## Proposed action

MFCL classified the item as:

`HOLD - PURPOSE MISMATCH`

The system preserved the evidence trail and routed the exception for the responsible municipal role. It did not invent missing authority or propose an automatic posting.

## Human approval

No approval was permitted while the purpose conflict remained. A preparer could not self-approve or override the restriction test through system access.

## Outcome

The unsupported use remained on hold. A separate synthetic capital-improvement transaction with aligned purpose and evidence advanced to required-role approval, demonstrating that the control distinguishes supported activity from unsupported activity rather than blocking all restricted-fund spending.

## Why it matters

The value is not autonomous posting. The value is identifying the conflict earlier, preserving source authority, explaining the reason, and placing the decision with the accountable human role.
